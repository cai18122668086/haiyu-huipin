# Product Presentation Decision Core

Use this core for every category. Keep concrete angles, colors and scene
materials in category or product-instance adapters.

## Decision order

1. Read current-product identity and verified facts.
2. Assign the page role and information-density mode.
3. Select one or two identity-defining or claim-defining visible targets.
4. Enumerate only views supported by current-product evidence.
5. Score each view before choosing composition.
6. Route the background from category, material, selling point, scene and
   campaign style.
7. Check local product/background separation at every key silhouette zone.
8. Compile typography and graphic devices without covering product evidence.

Before generation, compile a proof-success contract for every feature claim:
required subjects, required physical or spatial relation, target component,
thumbnail success condition, and forbidden symbolic substitutes. Block the
page when the selected expression cannot make the claim visually evident.
This is open synthesis, not a method menu: reason from the verified claim,
buyer objection and current-product evidence, then resolve one concrete view,
state/subject relation, spatial topology, copy topology, background and
lighting before calling the image model. Layout is the result of that
reasoning.

Every label, pointer, macro, glow and highlight must bind to the exact visible
current-product component, state or relation it names. Remove the device when
the target is unsupported, hidden or ambiguous.

When proof requires a person to contact or operate an identity-sensitive
product, require product-preserving compositing or masked editing. Keep the
original product geometry authoritative and place the person/scene around it.
Block a page when the relation is clear but the target component's shape,
material, attachment or relative position has changed.

## View score

Score candidate views using:

- product identity clarity: 30%;
- visible area of the selected feature target: 20%;
- complete-product coverage: 15%;
- non-overlap of identity-defining components: 10%;
- credible scene interaction: 10%;
- compositional harmony: 15%.

Do not prescribe one universal front, side or three-quarter view. Category
adapters may nominate candidates, but the highest evidence-supported score
wins.

## First-page boundary

Use the first page to establish product identity in one credible primary
scene. Allow `BRAND_ONLY`, `IDENTITY_PLUS_CATEGORY` or
`IDENTITY_PLUS_ONE_BENEFIT`. Reserve macros, parameter grids, multi-scene
proof and component callouts for later pages unless the product cannot be
identified without them.

Show an identity-defining feature naturally in the complete hero when
possible. Do not turn the opening page into a detail-proof page.
Use one scene, no detached proof module by default, and no more than two copy
groups. Move macros, component callouts, multi-scene comparisons and parameter
evidence to later pages.

## Background compatibility

First select a scene compatible with category, material, verified use,
selling point and Campaign Style. Then check:

- the product remains the first fixation;
- each key edge has sufficient local luminance or chroma separation;
- dark props do not sit behind dark product regions;
- transparent, reflective or pale products retain readable edges;
- separation comes from background value, light and shadow, never product
  recoloring.

Move, soften or remove competing props before changing the product.

## Reference roles

- `PRODUCT_IDENTITY_AUTHORITY`: original same-product evidence only.
- `STYLE_REFERENCE_ONLY`: a user-approved generated page may transfer
  palette, lighting, typography roles, graphic geometry and photography
  finish only.
- `CAMERA_GRAMMAR_ONLY`: an external reference may nominate framing or view
  grammar only.
- `NON_BINDING_REFERENCE`: may inspire exploration but cannot control facts,
  product structure or final selection.

Never allow a style or camera reference to import another product's color,
components, claims, offers, certifications or accessories.

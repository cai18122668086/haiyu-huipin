# High-risk proof conditions

Use this gate for before/after, time-series, human-body, expert, certificate, test, measured-result, comparison, sterilization, efficacy, safety, comfort, durability, noise, temperature, battery, and quantified performance claims.

## Condition tuple

Do not plan a high-risk proof until the available evidence identifies the applicable parts of:

`subject/sample + product/configuration + method/action + duration + environment + measurement + comparator + source`

Preserve every supplied qualifier. Never turn a qualified observation into a universal promise.

## Proof levels

1. `DOCUMENTED_RESULT`: a readable supplied report, certificate, test record, or authoritative product document supports the exact conclusion and conditions.
2. `VERIFIED_COMPARISON`: current-product evidence supports both compared states under materially equivalent conditions.
3. `OBSERVED_ACTION`: supplied imagery supports the action or product state, but not a measured effect.
4. `MECHANISM_ONLY`: documentation supports a component or working principle, but not the claimed outcome.
5. `UNSUPPORTED`: neither current-product evidence nor safe context supports the claim.

Use the highest truthful level, not the most dramatic one.

## Downgrade ladder

When evidence is incomplete:

- quantified result -> qualified mechanism or verified parameter;
- before/after -> same-product use action or material state without outcome claim;
- time-series result -> process sequence without a promised time or magnitude;
- medical/physiological outcome -> comfort, operation, or general-use context that does not imply treatment;
- competitor comparison -> current-product feature explanation or neutral selection criteria;
- unreadable certificate -> factual copy naming only what another readable source supports;
- expert endorsement -> documented design/process fact without borrowed authority;
- installation guarantee -> visible placement context plus explicit confirmation requirement.

Do not delete a useful verified fact merely because its strongest visual proof is unavailable. Change the claim strength and proof form.

## Same-condition comparison

Before using split-screen, before/after, or time progression, confirm that the compared objects share the relevant product, material, amount, angle, lighting, duration, environment, and measurement method. Disclose material differences. If equivalence cannot be established, do not imply a causal comparison.

## Document presentation

- Lead with the buyer conclusion, then show the readable source, method, conditions, and product anchor.
- Do not use an unreadable document thumbnail as a trust badge.
- Do not recreate a reference certificate, seal, report number, institution, graph, or measured value.
- Keep documentation secondary to the current product and the exact supported conclusion.

## Human and body evidence

- Use credible posture, contact point, direction, and product scale.
- Do not invent anatomy, skin/hair/teeth condition, pain, treatment response, or demographic suitability.
- Avoid exaggerated irritation, injury, disease, shame, or impossible body geometry.
- Treat model emotion as context, never as measured efficacy.

## Planning gate

For each high-risk module record the proof level, source, preserved qualifiers, comparison conditions if any, and downgrade route. If the source cannot be named from the current product's evidence inventory, the module cannot use a specific result or number.

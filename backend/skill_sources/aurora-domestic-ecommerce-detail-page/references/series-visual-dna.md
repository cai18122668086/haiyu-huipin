# Series visual DNA

## Purpose

Separate campaign continuity from page composition. Freeze the visual language after the first approved key visual, but do not freeze its layout.

Think of the relationship as:

`stable visual DNA + variable page grammar = one campaign with real rhythm`

## Visual passport

Record these campaign-wide tokens before generation:

- brand anchor: authorized logo asset, permitted variants, color, clear space, minimum readable scale and preferred zones;
- palette: primary, secondary, accent and neutral roles rather than isolated color names;
- typography: Chinese display, supporting, body and numeric character, weight contrast and permitted expressive behavior;
- graphic language: icon stroke, annotation line, corner radius, border, card and diagram treatment;
- photography: realism, lens feeling, depth, light direction, color temperature, shadow softness and retouching level;
- product rendering: silhouette, label fidelity, material finish, product scale and product/background separation;
- spatial system: safe margins, negative-space behavior, information surfaces and seam strategy;
- recurring motifs: only brand-owned or newly created shapes, lines, textures and framing devices.

The style master is the **written passport**, never a raster. Do not pass any
generated screen — the first screen included — as an image reference to a later
screen. A full-frame raster always carries its layout skeleton and its own
product drift into the next render: the 2026-09-17 trash-bin campaign inherited
a slightly off-model M01 into every later pair this way, and the drift compounded
screen by screen. Style continuity travels through the passport tokens compiled
into the prompt, the original product evidence, and the bottom edge strip from
`continuity_edge_reference`. Record `style_master_transfer_scope` to document
which passport tokens (palette, lighting, photographic finish, typography
character, graphic language, product rendering, recurring motifs) carry the
campaign; the spatial structure families below stay variable per screen.

## Brand-anchor system

- Two brand-anchor modes exist and are locked in the identity contract. A `BRANDED` campaign uses exactly one independent brand logo: it is mandatory on the first screen and must visibly match a readable user-supplied current-product brand mark. A tiny package logo is not a substitute for this first-screen mark. A `BRANDLESS` campaign (white-label product, confirmed by the user and recorded as `brandless_evidence`) uses zero independent logos anywhere and anchors the first screen through the real product subject, truthful category/specification lettering and the campaign visual DNA. Never invent, hallucinate or approximate a brand mark in either mode.
- From the second screen onward, do not add an independent, floating, corner, header, footer, watermark or repeated logo, including on trust, major-value and closing screens.
- A logo physically printed on verified product packaging is part of product identity. Keep it naturally visible when the package is shown; do not erase it, detach it from the package, enlarge it into a badge, or count it as an independent repeated logo.
- Maintain brand recognition after the first screen through palette roles, typography character, graphic language, photography, product rendering, material finish and recurring motifs rather than logo repetition.
- On the first screen, preserve the authorized logo's variant, proportions, color treatment and clear space. Count independent logos separately from logos printed on product packaging.
- Never redraw, translate, stylize or hallucinate a logo from an unreadable source.

## Continuity budget

Every module must explicitly inherit at least two visible passport tokens, normally more. Choose from color role, type character, lighting, surface material, annotation language, product rendering, brand anchor and recurring motif.

Changing composition, camera, spatial zones or text placement is not a style change when these tokens remain intact. Copying M01's layout is not continuity.

## Drift conditions

Treat a module as campaign drift when it changes several of the following without an approved replan:

- palette roles;
- typography character;
- light and photographic finish;
- graphic and card language;
- product rendering style;
- brand-mark behavior;
- perceived price tier or emotional tone.

Repair drift by restoring passport tokens while preserving the module's approved composition and proof direction.

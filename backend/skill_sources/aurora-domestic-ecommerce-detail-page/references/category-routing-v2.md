# Category routing (v4.1)

Route by product topology and buyer decision, not by category label alone. These patterns extend the shared storyboard grammar; they do not create category-specific fixed templates.

## Product topology

Classify every visible commercial entity before planning:

- `HERO`: the exact primary item being sold;
- `COMPANION`: another sellable unit whose function completes a verified system;
- `ACCESSORY`: a supplied part used, installed, charged, stored, or replaced with the hero;
- `VARIANT`: the same commercial role with a verified difference such as size, color, formula, mode, or configuration;
- `CONTEXT_ONLY`: a prop, environment, person, garment, food, pet, or device that is not included.

Keep the hero visually dominant unless the module duty is explicitly system relationship or selection. Never imply that a context-only object is included.

## Multi-device and suite products

Use a suite relationship screen only when the items have a documented joint role.

- Establish hero/companion roles and whether purchase is bundled, optional, or separately configured.
- Show topology before isolated parameters: side-by-side, stacked, connected, sequential, or shared-task relationships.
- Translate each unit's capacity or function into its part of the household/task workflow.
- Keep accessories and variants from competing with the primary suite identity.
- Use installation and fit routing when stacking, plumbing, power, ventilation, or clearance affects purchase.

## SKU, mode, and accessory choice

- Separate identity overview from functional selection when one screen would overload both.
- Match each option to one buyer condition or task using a selection matrix.
- Treat colors as visual choice, modes as operating choice, accessories as task extensions, and bundles as commercial configuration.
- Never invent option availability, compatibility, included quantity, or bundle contents.

## Human-action products

For apparel, personal care, beauty devices, handheld appliances, sports goods, and body-contact products, plan the visible action before styling the person.

Record:

- contact point and product orientation;
- hand/body path or range of motion;
- required body scale and camera distance;
- garment or material deformation relevant to the claim;
- safe result boundary and prohibited inference.

Use a close or medium view when contact and operation are the proof. Use a wider scene only when environment or whole-body movement is the buyer question.

## Apparel and movement

- Prove fit with body silhouette, seam position, garment length, and movement, not with a floating garment alone.
- Prove stretch, recovery, breathability, or moisture behavior only through supported material/action evidence.
- Keep styling inspiration separate from size/fit proof.
- Use the same body pose and conditions for any fit comparison that implies superiority.

## Food, pet, beauty, durable goods regression rule

Do not force these new routes onto ordinary sensory, ingredient, material, structure, or scene modules. Continue using the established visual-proof route when a single product and a single visible relation answer the buyer question.

## Planning checklist

Before compiling the storyboard, confirm:

1. Every visible entity has a topology role.
2. The buyer can tell what is included.
3. System, selection, installation, and body-action questions are separated when they need different evidence.
4. Each module still has one lead conclusion.
5. No category pattern has dictated a fixed screen count, order, ratio, palette, or page skeleton.

# Detail story grammar

## v4.1 information progression

Every v4.1 plan adds a unique M01 hero contract and an information contract. Each module answers one buyer question, names concrete new information, and states the purchase judgment it enables. Each continuous Pair must add visible understanding; a repeated overview, feature-word stack, or decorative poster variation is not detail-page coverage.

In addition to fact-cluster coverage, every normal sales page carries a pain-to-solution story obligation. Use the category-neutral contract in `pain-solution-visual-story.md`: make the undesired state visible, connect it to a verified product value bridge, vary semantic visual carriers across the page, and reserve copy-only expression for genuinely text-verifiable material. The resolved screen-count decision must state how it preserves this obligation.

Use `selling-point-depth-and-count.md` before selecting the count. Coverage answers “did we mention it?”; depth answers “did the buyer receive enough distinct reasons, scenes or proof to act?”. The selected count must satisfy both.

## Purchase-decision map

Start from the selected `operating_strategy`: target buyer, primary pain or doubt, chosen positioning, core promise and priority verified claims. Pain and audience hypotheses may guide scenarios and buyer questions, but only verified facts may become factual product claims.

Classify the current product by dominant and supporting logic, not category name alone:

- effect or parameter;
- material or sensory experience;
- structure or durability;
- identity or trust;
- price or specification;
- scene or emotion.
- interaction or control;
- installation or compatibility;
- selection or configuration;
- suite or multi-entity relationship.

List the buyer's most consequential doubts. Each selected module must answer one doubt, advance one conclusion, or close one transaction risk.

## Candidate module duties

These are selectable duties, not a fixed order:

- first-screen value promise;
- core difference;
- mechanism or structure proof;
- material, ingredient or detail proof;
- operation or use process;
- result or experience;
- audience and scenario fit;
- parameter-to-benefit explanation;
- fact-supported comparison or selection reason;
- trust and risk removal;
- SKU, package, compatibility, service or offer closure;
- final value reinforcement.
- directed pathway or process explanation;
- trigger–feedback–result interaction;
- installation, fit or maintenance-access proof;
- buyer-condition-to-option selection;
- suite topology and role explanation.

## Planning rules

- Resolve material contradictions before building claim coverage, screen-count decision, style, or storyboard. Ask once with all conflicts and wait for the user's retained values; planning against competing values is invalid.
- Set module count from selling-point depth and evidence, selecting exactly 8, 10, or 12 screens. Eight is the lower bound; choose 10 or 12 when primary claims need separate mechanism, use/result, selection, trust or closure depth. Counts outside 8/10/12 are invalid for this skill.
- Build a complete claim-coverage map before choosing the count. Every verified fact must be marked lead, support, context-only, or deferred with a reason.
- Select a must-remember claim set instead of treating every fact as commercially equal. At least one priority claim must establish the first-screen value promise and at least one must return in the final reinforcement; each must-remember claim needs visible proof or truthful qualification in the body.
- Derive one direct screen count after depth analysis. Do not present alternative compact/balanced/full-sales candidates; choose exactly 8, 10 or 12 screens and record why one fewer or one additional pair would weaken the approved story.
- Record the exact merge or separation decisions that produced the final count. A bare statement such as “8 screens is enough” is invalid.
- Use one lead conclusion per module. Combine related supporting points when they form one coherent picture.
- Never repeat the same claim with the same proof and buyer conclusion in adjacent modules.
- Follow a dense rational module with a lower-density scene, sensory, or result module when possible.
- Translate parameters into purchase meaning.
- Keep lower modules commercially finished; do not let them decay into document attachments.
- End with specification, service, offer, trust, or a reinforced buying reason.
- Preserve relevant supplied formula, ingredient, process, use and specification content by grouping it into coherent modules; do not confuse concise selection with broad deletion.
- Treat “visual first” as a division of labor: the image demonstrates the object, action, relation, change, scale, texture or evidence; concise copy names the takeaway, disambiguates the relation, or states an approved figure/condition. Normal sales modules must not be reduced to either a poster-sized headline or a silent decorative picture.
- For every module schedule a composition family, camera scale, text mode, reading path, brand anchor and declared variation from the previous module. Set the independent-logo policy to `REQUIRED_IF_AUTHORIZED` for M01 and `FORBIDDEN` for M02 onward in a `BRANDED` campaign, and to `FORBIDDEN` for every module including M01 in a `BRANDLESS` white-label campaign. Later brand anchors must be expressed through visual DNA and product identity, never a repeated standalone logo.
- A centered display headline is valid when it serves the module duty. It is not the default structure for every module.
- Route multi-device, accessory, variant, apparel, and body-contact products through `category-routing-v2.md` (the v4.1 category-routing reference) before fixing module duties.
- Route pathway, interaction, installation, and selection duties through `advanced-page-archetypes.md`.
- Apply `high-risk-proof-conditions.md` before selecting any quantified result, comparison, time sequence, body claim, expert, certificate, or test proof.

## Transition contract

For every module record:

1. What the previous module established.
2. What question the current module answers.
3. What new question the buyer will naturally have afterward.
4. How the next module receives that question.
